## Non-obvious trade-offs
- **Identity and ownership (I11):** the overlay renders the Middleware, both routes and the Certificate in namespace architecture; only apps-architecture renders them, and the Namespace object stays in k8s/platform (owned by platform), so no identity has two Flux owners.
- **Readiness:** with `wait: true` the Certificate counts toward apps-architecture's readiness, so the Kustomization stays not-Ready until the staging certificate issues; nothing depends on it (research Pitfall 7), and `healthChecks` are omitted because Flux ignores them when `wait` is true.
- **Edge labels:** the site gets exactly one logical relationship per pair (traefik to archsite, flux to archsite, archsite to dockerhub); a second relationship on a drawn pair with a different title would merge to `[...]` and fail the label rule. The existing `ci -> dockerhub` relationship is re-titled rather than duplicated.
- **Security:** no rate-limit Middleware on the site's route (static files, as on nonprod); the container holds no secret and mounts no token. The Certificate is staging, so a mistake costs no production rate limit.
- **State invalidation:** drill-output.txt's DRIFT header carries element and workload counts, which this commit changes, so the committed drill-output.txt no longer matches a fresh replay until plan 05 regenerates it. Nothing in the hook or CI compares the two, and nothing is pushed between the plans; plan 05's exit-code comparison against the file as committed before this plan also shows this commit changed no earlier drill's outcome.
- **File count (17 paths, above the 15-file band):** accepted by orchestrator, iteration 2: the atomic-commit argument and the per-file reason table above hold it at 14 source files plus three records, at 82k estimated tokens, under the 100k budget; the plan is not split.
</tradeoffs>
