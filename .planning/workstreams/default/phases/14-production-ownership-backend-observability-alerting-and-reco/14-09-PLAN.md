<objective>
Stand up Tempo with 7-day retention on the VM disk, connect it to Grafana (logs to traces and back), switch on OTLP export from both app pods, measure Tempo's memory, and check the node still has headroom, trimming in the order D-09 fixes if it does not.

Purpose: D-07..D-09 and the measurable outcome "one request, followed end to end": a log line leads to its trace and the trace crosses Redpanda.
Output: HelmRelease tempo with measured limits, Grafana datasources, per-environment export env, a recorded headroom verdict.
</objective>

<data_flow>
Each app pod's OTLP exporter batches spans and POSTs them to Service tempo:4318 in monitoring; Tempo writes them to its WAL and blocks on the local-path PVC and drops blocks older than 168 hours. Grafana queries Tempo on 3200 through the new datasource; the Loki datasource's derived field turns the traceId inside each JSON log line into a link to that trace, and the Tempo datasource links back to Loki.
</data_flow>

<approaches>
| Approach | Pros / Cons | Why picked |
|---|---|---|
| A. App exports OTLP directly to Tempo 3.2.0 monolithic from grafana-community (research recommendation) | Pro: no extra hop; Alloy stays at its measured 40-75Mi floor; switching to a collector later is an endpoint change. Con: no tail sampling or buffering beyond the SDK's batch queue | Chosen (Claude's discretion on the OTLP receiver) |
| B. OTLP into the existing Alloy DaemonSet, Alloy forwards to Tempo | Pro: one collector for logs and traces, room for processors. Con: Alloy must expose 4317/4318 through chart values not yet verified, and its memory ladder must be redone | Rejected for now |
| C. Deprecated grafana/tempo chart 1.24.4 (Tempo 2.9) | Pro: older, widely deployed. Con: flagged deprecated in the Grafana index | Rejected; 3.2.0 is also the first 3.x that enforces retention |

Non-obvious trade-offs:
- Chart 3.2.0 was published 2026-10-14; if it fails to install, the fallback is 3.1.0 with explicit block retention, because 3.0.0 to 3.1.0 silently keep 14 days.
- 100% sampling of a low-traffic app is small on disk at 7 days; the 10Gi PVC is generous and the default NodeFilesystem alerts watch the disk.
- Changing app env rolls the pod (Recreate): one short restart per environment; no Flux suspend is needed because the probes do not change.
- TRACING_EXPORT_ENABLED lives in the overlays, not the base, so turning one environment off never creates a duplicate env entry.
</approaches>
